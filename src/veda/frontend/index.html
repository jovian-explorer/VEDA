📖 Step-by-Step Planetary Data Processing and Visualization Guide

Welcome to the VEDA Planetary Science Data Laboratory. This interactive guide walks you through the complete end-to-end scientific pipeline: discovering authentic mission archives, loading raw PDS3 tables and astronomical FITS images, deriving atmospheric thermodynamics and ionospheric metrics, comparing multi-spacecraft soundings on unified grids, and exporting publication-ready figures.

Stage 1: Planetary Target Discovery and Archive Acquisition

Data Discovery

Missions archive their raw and calibrated products in official repositories. VEDA reads their catalogues directly:

  • NASA PDS Atmospheres Node: Magellan, Pioneer Venus Orbiter radio occultations, Venus Express, Mars Global Surveyor, Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (radio occultation T-P; Mars Climate Sounder temperature and aerosol profiles; CRISM dust and water-ice aerosol profiles; aerobraking density profiles), Mars Odyssey (aerobraking density profiles), MAVEN (accelerometer density profiles), the Mars entry profiles of Spirit, Opportunity, Phoenix, Curiosity and InSight, the VEGA 2 lander descent, Galileo probe, Huygens HASI, Cassini (Titan ionosphere, PDS4) and Juno MWR.
  • NASA PDS PPI Node: MAVEN ROSE ionospheric electron density profiles.
  • ESA Planetary Science Archive (PSA): Mars Express MaRS and Venus Express VeRa, and every Mars Express, Venus Express, Rosetta and BepiColombo instrument by date.
  • Research data repositories (Zenodo, BIRA-IASB): Venus Express VeRa and SPICAV-SOIR profiles published by the teams outside PSA.
  • JAXA DARTS: Akatsuki radio occultation (L2 to L4), plus Akatsuki SPICE kernels for geometry.
  • ISRO ISSDC / PRADAN: Mars Orbiter Mission and Chandrayaan-2; sign in on the PRADAN website, download, then import into VEDA.

By Celestial Body → Find observations of …: give a date range and VEDA lists every product from every connected mission for that body. By Planetary Mission → Archive data: choose payloads and data sets, filter by date, product type or text, and download. The first search reads the archive's index (needs the internet once); downloads are cached for offline use.

Stage 2: Planetary Data Ingestion and File Formats

File Ingestion

Planetary observations are distributed in specialized scientific formats designed for long-term preservation:

  • PDS3 Labels and Tables (.lbl + .tab): The PDS3 standard pairs an ASCII label (.lbl) containing metadata and column definitions (e.g. ^TABLE = "filename.tab", START_BYTE, BYTES) with a fixed-width or comma-delimited data table (.tab). VEDA's parser automatically handles pointer indirection and column offsets.
  • Astronomical FITS Images (.fit / .fits): Flexible Image Transport System files organize astronomical images into Header Data Units (HDUs). Headers provide scientific coordinate keys (DATE-OBS, EXPTIME, FILTER, DISTANCE), while data arrays preserve raw 16-bit or 32-bit floating point CCD photon counts.
  • PDS4 Labels (.xml) and Tables: PDS4 products describe their character, delimited or binary tables in an XML label; select the label and its data file together.
  • Text Tables (.csv, .txt, .dat, .asc): comma, tab, semicolon or space separated, with or without a header row. Before a table is read, VEDA shows its columns with their units and ranges and asks what each one is (altitude, temperature, pressure, densities, uncertainties, geometry) and in which unit, and which body, mission, instrument and time the file belongs to.

Stage 3: Scientific Thermodynamics and Diagnostic Derivations

Physics Engine

When a 1D vertical sounding is ingested, VEDA's analytical engine couples the observation with body-specific constants (planetary radius $R_p$, surface gravity $g_0$, molecular weight $\mu$, specific gas constant $R_{spec} = R_{univ}/\mu$, and heat capacity $C_p$) to compute key atmospheric diagnostics:

1. Altitude-Dependent Planetary Gravity:

$$g(z) = g_0 \left(\frac{R_p}{R_p + z}\right)^2$$

On Mars, Jupiter and Saturn, the effective gravity with $J_2$ and rotation at the profile's latitude; the MGS and MRO radio occultations are integrated in the geopotential their archives give.
2. Environmental Lapse Rate:

$$\Gamma(z) = -\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}$$

computed using central difference gradients on finite valid data points.
3. Poisson Potential Temperature ($\theta$):

$$\theta(z) = T(z) \left(\frac{P_0}{P(z)}\right)^{\frac{R_{spec}}{C_p}}$$

where $P_0$ is the reference pressure level of the planetary body (e.g. 92,000 hPa for Venus, 6.1 hPa for Mars).
4. Brunt-Vaisala Static Stability Frequency ($N^2$):

$$N^2(z) = \frac{g(z)}{T(z)} \left(\frac{dT}{dz} + \Gamma_d\right) = \frac{g(z)}{\theta(z)} \frac{d\theta}{dz}$$

where dry adiabatic lapse rate $\Gamma_d = g(z) / C_p$. Regions with $N^2 > 0$ are stably stratified against buoyancy oscillations; regions with $N^2 < 0$ are dynamically unstable to convection.
5. Ionospheric Total Electron Content (VTEC):

$$\text{VTEC} = 10^{-7} \int_{z_{base}}^{z_{top}} N_e(z)\,dz \quad [\text{TECU}]$$

integrating radio occultation electron density profiles (e.g. Mars Express MaRS, Mars Global Surveyor, Cassini RSS at Titan).
6. CO$_2$ Frost Point (Mars and Venus):

$$T_{CO_2} = \frac{3182.48\ \text{K}}{23.3494 - \ln(x_{CO_2}\,p / 1\ \text{hPa})}$$

(James et al. 1992, as written by Greve et al. 2010), with $x_{CO_2}$ the CO$_2$ volume fraction (Mars 0.951, Venus 0.965). Where $T - T_{CO_2} \le 0$, CO$_2$ can condense: polar-night snowfall on Mars, the cold layer near 125 km at the Venus terminator.

Stage 4: Multi-Mission Aligned Comparative Analysis

Comparative Science

Different spacecraft observe targets at different epochs, spatial coordinates, and vertical resolutions. VEDA overcomes this by interpolating soundings onto a uniform planetary vertical grid:

  • Which profiles: choose them by date, latitude, local time, solar zenith angle or Mars season (Ls) and the number per mission; VEDA searches the whole archive and reports why profiles were left out.
  • Common grid: profiles are interpolated (never extrapolated) onto altitude levels (default 0.5 km on Venus, Mars and Pluto, 2 km elsewhere; set Grid step) or onto pressure levels uniform in log pressure, and if you like smoothed to a common vertical resolution (error-weighted, with the uncertainty propagated).
  • Composite mean $\mu(z)$ and spread ($\pm 1\sigma$): arithmetic for temperature-like quantities, geometric (in log space) for pressure and densities; per group for climatologies by latitude, local time, zenith angle, Ls, year, month, month of the year, mission or instrument, as deviations from the mean, and as differences between groups with 95 % bootstrap intervals, also from coincident pairs only (profiles of two groups close in time and place, to compare instruments on the same atmosphere).
  • Uncertainties: each profile's 1σ (archived or propagated) and, for Mars Express, Akatsuki and Pioneer Venus, the systematic uncertainty from the temperature assumed at the top of the retrieval; the mean with its standard error or 95 % bootstrap interval, optionally weighted by 1/σ², and an uncertainty budget at each level (natural variability against random errors).
  • Sampling: where and when the compared profiles were taken (latitude against Ls or date and against local time), since differences between missions or groups can come from their sampling.
  • Reference atmospheres: Venus-GRAM 2021 / VIRA on Venus and Mars-GRAM 2024 on Mars, as a line or as each profile's deviation from it.
  • Altitude cut: one point per profile at a fixed altitude, a layer statistic or a whole-profile diagnostic, against time, latitude, local time, zenith angle, Ls, longitude or day of year, with tide and wave fits and means in bins (seasonal or latitude climatologies) with bootstrap intervals.
  • Any variable: temperature, pressure, densities, potential temperature, $N^2$, lapse rate, temperature from density, the margin above the CO$_2$ frost point and more, in the units you choose.

Stage 5: Astronomical Camera Imaging and Photometric Transects

Imaging and Transects

Planetary camera observations (such as the bundled Akatsuki UVI image) capture spatial structures like cloud bands, atmospheric waves, volcanic plumes, and impact craters. VEDA provides astronomical image tools:

  • Astronomical Contrast Stretches: ZScale (IRAF standard), Percentile (0.5% to 99.5%), Linear, Logarithmic, Square Root, Asinh and Histogram Equalized.
  • False-Color Palettes: Inferno, Viridis, Plasma, Magma, Twilight and Grayscale colormaps to highlight subtle intensity gradients.
  • Interactive 1D Photometric Line Transects: Click and drag across the image canvas to slice arbitrary cross-sections $(x_0, y_0) \to (x_1, y_1)$ with sub-pixel bilinear interpolation.
  • Pixel Histograms and CDF: Inspect dynamic range and pixel intensity distributions.

Stage 6: Scientific Export, Provenance, and Academic Citation

Publication Ready

VEDA ensures full scientific provenance and reproducible workflows:

  • CSV exports: a profile with its derived quantities, a comparison on its common grid (with a recipe line to redo it), or every compared profile with all variables at its own levels; the headers name the archive, source file, citation and VEDA version.
  • Structured JSON Export: Delivers complete profile records with derived physics for automated downstream analysis in Python or Julia.
  • Figures: the publication figure at the DPI set in Settings (300 by default), and PNG or SVG at a journal's column width with Export figure.
  • Citation: Please cite the data set and the instrument team's reference publication (listed under Data & Licenses) and the software: Keshav Aggarwal (2026), VEDA, Zenodo, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23215291.

Mathematical Formulations of Planetary Atmospheric Profiling

Planetary Physics & Inversion

How the archived radio occultation profiles are retrieved (1), and the relations VEDA uses for its derived quantities (2 to 6):

1. Radio Occultation Abel Inversion (instrument team retrievals)

The Doppler frequency shift provides the ray bending angle \( \alpha(a) \). For oblate planets (Jupiter, Saturn), the standard spherical Abel transform is generalized using the local radius of curvature \( R_c(\phi, \theta) \):

$$n(r) = \exp\left[ \frac{1}{\pi} \int_a^\infty \frac{\alpha(x)}{\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}} \, dx \right], \quad N(r) = (n(r) - 1) \times 10^6$$

Reference: Fjeldbo, Kliore & Eshleman (1971), Astron. J., 76, 123-140. DOI: 10.1086/111096

2. Variable Planetary Gravity & Scale Height

Planetary gravity decreases with height above the reference body radius \( R_{body} \). The local pressure scale height \( H(z) \) depends on specific gas constant \( R_{spec} = R_{univ}/\mu \):

$$g(z) = g_0 \left(\frac{R_{body}}{R_{body} + z}\right)^2, \quad H(z) = \frac{R_{spec} T(z)}{g(z)}$$

Reference: Chamberlain & Hunten (1987), Theory of Planetary Atmospheres, Academic Press.

3. Hydrostatic Integration & Mass Density

Hydrostatic balance links pressure \( P(z) \), mass density \( \rho(z) \) and temperature; the radio science teams integrate it downwards from refractivity, and VEDA from measured densities (accelerometers, entry probes, SOIR) to give temperature and pressure:

$$P(z) = \int_z^\infty \rho(z') g(z') \, dz', \quad \rho(z) = \frac{P(z)}{R_{spec} T(z)}$$

Reference: Tellmann et al. (2012), Icarus, 221(2), 471-480. DOI: 10.1016/j.icarus.2012.08.023

4. Poisson Potential Temperature on Other Worlds

Potential temperature \( \theta \) scales temperature to reference pressure \( P_{ref} \) using the body-specific Poisson exponent \( \kappa = R_{spec}/c_p \):

$$\theta(z) = T(z) \left(\frac{P_{ref}}{P(z)}\right)^\kappa, \quad \kappa = \frac{R_{spec}}{c_p}$$

Reference: Holton & Hakim (2012), An Introduction to Dynamic Meteorology.

5. Brunt-Vaisala Static Stability & Buoyancy Period

Vertical static stability is measured by \( N^2 \). When \( N^2 > 0 \), fluid parcels oscillate with period \( \tau_B \):

$$N^2(z) = \frac{g(z)}{T(z)} \left(\frac{dT}{dz} + \frac{g(z)}{c_p}\right) = \frac{g(z)}{\theta(z)} \frac{d\theta}{dz}, \quad \tau_B = \frac{2\pi}{N}$$

Reference: Tellmann et al. (2012), Icarus, 221, 471-480.

6. Planetary Chapman Ionospheric Electron Density

Photo-ionization in planetary upper atmospheres is parameterized by the Chapman production function with normalized height \( z_r = (z - h_m)/H_p \):

$$N_e(z) = N_{max} \cdot \exp\left[ \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{z - h_m}{H_p} - \exp\left(-\frac{z - h_m}{H_p}\right) \right) \right]$$

Reference: Chapman (1931), Proc. Phys. Soc., 43(1), 26-45. DOI: 10.1088/0959-5309/43/1/305

Open science
