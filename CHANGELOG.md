# Changelog

All notable changes to VEDA. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/): 0.x releases are public and in active development (feedback from users shapes them), 1.0.0 will mark a stable interface.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-02, updated continuously)

Changes go to the main branch continuously and are released as "VEDA 0.2.0 build N" about once a month (until 7 October 2026 every tested change was published straight away), so this log lists changes by version and each build contains the changes made before it. Only the latest and the previous release can be downloaded from GitHub; every release stays archived on Zenodo under the concept DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23215291](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23215291).

**In short.** New data sets (MRO Mars Climate Sounder profiles, MAVEN accelerometer densities and ROSE electron density profiles, MRO and Mars Odyssey aerobraking densities, MRO CRISM aerosol profiles, Venus Express VeRa and SOIR profiles from research repositories); comparisons that choose their own profiles, group them into climatologies, line them up on altitude or pressure and compare instruments on coincident pairs; an altitude cut with layer statistics, per-profile diagnostics and tide and wave fits; temperature and pressure retrieved from density profiles and a hydrostatic consistency check; body constants (molar mass, gas constant, heat capacity) taken from measured compositions; latitude-dependent gravity on Jupiter and Saturn; a dialog for loading your own tables; parallel reading and downloading; automatic builds with update notices. Several corrections change published numbers; they are marked **Fix** below and the size of each change is given.

### Data sets
- **MAVEN ROSE electron density profiles** (PDS4 bundle urn:nasa:pds:maven.rose.derived, Withers, PDS PPI Node, doi:10.17189/1517630; Withers et al. 2020): 1756 ionospheric profiles from July 2016 to December 2025 with their 1-sigma, listed from the bundle's year and month folders. Each profile takes the team's representative point, where the ray passes 3550 km from the centre of Mars (time, latitude, longitude, local time and zenith angle of the header), instead of medians along a ray path that runs to 1000 km; data sets can now name such a point. The retrieval takes all refraction as the plasma's, so below about 80 km the neutral atmosphere gives large negative densities, which the archive's SIS calls not realistic: each profile is left out up to its highest level below 100 km with a density below -3 sigma. The files also run to 1000-2500 km, into the noise: above its peak (the largest density below 300 km) each profile ends at the first level under 2 sigma (225 km, median of 70 profiles), and a profile without such a peak above 2 sigma is left out (4 of the 70, at zenith angles of 83 to 112 deg). In 29 dayside profiles of 2016-2017 the peak density goes as cos^0.53+-0.05 of the zenith angle, the cos^0.5 of Chapman theory.
- **MAVEN accelerometer density profiles** (PDS4 bundle urn:nasa:pds:maven_acc, Tolson & Lugo, PDS Atmospheres Node; Zurek et al. 2015): thermospheric mass density along every periapsis pass from October 2014 to 2020 (10,299 passes: the deep dips to about 120 km, the 2019 aerobraking and the science orbits), each pass as its inbound and outbound leg at the periapsis time, with the 1-s density and its 1-sigma. The bundle has no index: its 71 month folders are listed (20,598 legs in 14 s), and the time comes from the label when a profile is read. Altitudes above the spheroid are put on the 3389.5 km sphere; longitudes are east (local solar time computed from them agrees with the file's to 0.02 h). Beyond 2 sigma the files hold noise about zero out to 200-230 km, so each leg stops at the first level below twice its 1-sigma: the deep dip of periapsis 3302 keeps 118.7 to 152.5 km (inbound) and 160.0 km (outbound), most science-orbit passes a few kilometres near 150-160 km. Checked against the archive's user advisory: the bias noise and peak densities of its seven example passes match the files (for example 0.008 against 0.0077611 kg/km^3 and a peak of 5.065 kg/km^3 for pass 3302). MAVEN's accelerometer is added to its instruments.
- **MRO Mars Climate Sounder profiles in comparisons.** Each row of the 4-hour MCS DDR files (about 300 profiles on 105 pressure surfaces, with temperature 1-sigma) is now a profile of its own (`<file>_P<row>`), read from the downloaded file, for comparisons, the altitude cut, the sampling map and the profile view; a filtered comparison reads up to 10 files spread over the dates and keeps the rows whose geometry passes. Altitudes go on VEDA's Mars sphere from the surface point's radius; the hydrostatic check uses the gravity MCS integrated with (GM/r^2): largest departure 0.06 % (median of 32 rows). Against the MRO radio occultations of December 2011 at 62-68 deg N and 3-4 h local time, the means on pressure levels differ by -1.3 to +0.6 K at 3-5 hPa and MCS is 2-4 K warmer at 0.1-2 hPa.
- **MRO aerobraking density profiles** (MRO-M-ACCEL-5-PROFILE-V1.0, volume MROA_0001, Tolson et al., PDS Atmospheres Node): thermospheric mass density from the accelerometer for each aerobraking pass, April to August 2006 (426 passes), from about 90 to 150 km, with 1-sigma uncertainties, latitude, longitude, local time and solar zenith angle per second (Tolson et al. 2008). As for Odyssey, each pass is an inbound and an outbound profile at the periapsis time. The 1-s density is used (the file's 39-s mean is kept "for continuity with aerobraking data bases"); -1 marks missing values. Altitudes are given above the areodetic spheroid (a = 3396.19 km, f = 5.886e-3) along its normal: VEDA turns them into radii with the latitude and puts them on the 3389.5 km sphere (13 km lower near the poles, 7 km higher at the equator than the archive's numbers) and makes the latitudes planetocentric. Checked on archive files of passes 30, 150, 250 and 420 (Ls within 0.02 deg of the archive's level 3 files); the raw accelerometer files of each pass, all named ACCEL.TAB and so on, are catalogued under the index's product ids instead of replacing one another.
- **Mars Odyssey aerobraking density profiles** (ODY-M-ACCEL-5-DERIVED-V1.0, Withers & Murphy, PDS Atmospheres Node): thermospheric mass density from about 85 to 170 km for each aerobraking pass, October 2001 to January 2002 (329 passes), from the accelerometer, with 1-sigma uncertainties, latitude, longitude, local time and solar zenith angle per level (Tolson et al. 2005). Each pass is offered as two profiles, the inbound and the outbound leg, which are hundreds of kilometres and up to 35 degrees of latitude apart; both carry the periapsis time. The 7-s running-mean density is used. The labels give the densities in kg/m^3 but the values are kg/km^3 (checked with the drag equation and the file's own acceleration, speed and drag coefficient); VEDA converts them. Altitudes are from the radial distance, on the 3389.5 km sphere used for the other Mars data sets. 2001 Mars Odyssey added as a mission.
- **MRO CRISM dust and water-ice aerosol profiles** (PDS Atmospheres Node, bundle mro-crism_atmos-db, Khayat 2024, doi:10.17189/76ha-be75), retrieved from CRISM limb observations: 502 profiles of dust and water-ice "mixing ratio" from 2009 to 2012 (Smith et al. 2013, optical depth at 2.2 µm per unit fraction of the column mass) and 916 profiles of dust and water-ice optical depth per mbar with dust and ice effective radii and their uncertainties from 2010 to 2017 (Guzewich et al. 2014, 2019), from near the surface to 50-60 km. The two definitions differ, so they are separate comparison variables. The tables give a Mars year and Ls but no time: VEDA dates each profile from them (the labels' start and stop dates match the first and last rows to within a day) and keeps only the date, so no local time is computed. Longitudes, given positive west, are converted to east. Heights are above the local surface. (The PDS Atmospheres Node did not answer while this was added; the files were checked on archived copies of the same URLs.)
- **Venus Express temperature profiles**, which PSA and PDS do not hold, from the teams' research data repositories (all CC-BY-4.0):
  - VeRa radio occultations of 2014 (NASA DSN), 25 profiles with time, latitude, longitude, solar zenith angle and local time (Gramigna et al. 2023; Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.20056665);
  - VeRa radio occultations 2006-2009 retrieved by Full Spectrum Inversion, 32 profiles with date, latitude and local time (Imamura et al. 2018; Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.4621070). Below the lowest valid level these files hold the number density constant, which made the derived temperatures wrong (800 K at 39 km); those rows are masked;
  - SPICAV-SOIR solar occultations 2006-2014, 644 profiles of CO2 density, pressure and temperature from about 70 to 170 km at the terminator, with uncertainties (Mahieux et al. 2015; BIRA-IASB doi:10.18758/71021089).
- **Fix: Venus Express FSI profiles too hot at their bottom.** Above the constant-density rows, each file keeps a few kilometres where the retrieved density hardly falls with height as the signal fades, so the temperature computed from it stayed at 400-580 K up to 55 km, falling 30-40 K per km (temperature_fsi_060827i-1 gave 399.7 K at 52.5 km where the other profiles give 285-331 K). Levels at the bottom where the temperature falls faster than 15 K/km (the dry adiabatic rate is about 10) are now masked as well; the profiles now start between 43.6 and 54.5 km, at 310-395 K. Profiles already downloaded are rewritten when next read.
- **Fix: SOIR temperatures held fixed at the top.** 156 of the 644 SOIR profiles repeat one temperature over their top 10 km (orbit 335: 226.2 K from 147 to 156 km): the value the retrieval starts its downward integration from, not a measurement, and their pressure there is computed with it. Temperature and pressure are now left out at those levels (the measured density is kept, so the temperature retrieved by VEDA from the density covers them). The top level of the other 488 profiles holds that starting temperature too, with an uncertainty of 1e-13 K (it gave those values almost all the weight in an inverse-variance composite); it is left out as well. Profiles already downloaded are rewritten when next read.
- **Fix: Mars Express ionosphere profiles kept the neutral atmosphere and the noise.** The MaRS electron density profiles run from the surface to 1000-5800 km and VEDA used every level, so the X-band profiles carried the neutral atmosphere's negative densities (to -1e6 cm^-3 near the surface) and all of them their noise far above the peak: in 36 profiles of 2004-2022 the peak search found 954 and 1117 km in two nightside profiles, and the Chapman fits put peaks at -19 to 55 km in six and at 854 and 1187 km in two. VEDA now reads the team's lowest valid altitude from each product's information text (^ION_INFO, now downloaded with the product; without a value, the ROSE rule: the highest level below 100 km at -3 sigma) and ends each profile above its peak at the first level under 2 sigma, as for ROSE. In those 36 profiles the lowest kept level is the team's in all 33 that give one (within the level spacing), the peak density and altitude are the team's in all 19 where the team gives a peak, the profiles end at 80 to 371 km, the Chapman peaks lie at 81 to 256 km, and one nightside profile with no peak above 2 sigma is left out.
- Venus Express instruments SPICAV-SOIR, SPICAV, ASPERA-4 and MAG added to the mission.
- New kinds of source: research data repositories (Zenodo and institutional archives), whose profiles have no PDS labels and are described by the data set (columns and units) and rewritten as small normalised CSV files; and repository tables holding many profiles (CRISM), which are split into one profile each.
- PDS3 tables whose label is at the top of the data file and whose columns are in a format file (^STRUCTURE) are read as profiles, and a table whose label puts the columns one byte off (so that "-73.1" was read as "-73.") is read field by field.
- PDS4 fixed-width tables whose label is off are read as written: a table offset pointing into the first record is taken back to the start of that line when the whole line is one record of the label's length, and when every line holds exactly one value per field, each on its field's columns, the values are read whole even where the label gives a field too narrow. The MAVEN accelerometer profiles need both (offset 1 for 0, and 8-character values in 7-character fields): the first row read -300 s as 300 s, the spacecraft mass lost a digit and the bias noise 0.008 kg/km^3 read as 0.00. The 29 PDS4 products in a test cache (Cassini, InSight, MSL, Pioneer Venus) read exactly as before.
- **Fix: densities labelled KILOGRAM PER CUBIC METER multiplied by 1000.** The test for g/cm^3 (Cassini) looked for "GRAM" and "CM" in the unit with its spaces removed, which "KILOGRAM PER CUBIC METER" also passes. Units are now read word by word; data sets whose labels write KG/M**3 or g/cm^3 were not affected.
- **Fix: uncertainties of -1.** The Phoenix entry profile writes -1 in its uncertainty columns where it has no value; these were kept as uncertainties of -1 K, -0.01 hPa and -1 kg/m^3 (in the profile view, CSV exports and weighted fits). Negative uncertainties are now treated as missing.
- **Fix: made-up values on Akatsuki camera images.** When a header keyword VEDA looked for was absent, the image info used invented defaults: the UVI sample showed a 0.05 s exposure, 350,000 km from Venus and a 45 deg phase angle, where the file gives 0.5 s (EXPOSURE), 128,304 km (S_DISTAV) and 23.2 deg (S_SSCPHA, at the sub-spacecraft point). These keywords are read now and nothing is filled in when a file has none. Loaded FITS images no longer take the file's creation date (DATE) as the observation time, in the image list or in the loading dialog's answer; a time typed when loading is used for images as for profiles.
- **Fix: Mars Express "AIO"/"IIO" files were offered as profiles.** They are short text files describing each occultation's geometry and failed to load, using up part of every comparison; existing catalogues are corrected the next time VEDA starts.
- **Fix: bundled sample profiles missing on a first start.** The samples are copied into the catalogue on its first use, but the check for downloaded products ran before that, so a mission view or comparison opened first found none of them.
- **Fix: downloads broken off part-way started again from nothing.** A transfer that the server cut off failed the whole download; the BIRA-IASB repository cut the 38 MB SOIR file after 5 to 11 MB on every attempt, so the SOIR data set could not be indexed at all. A broken transfer is now continued from where it stopped (HTTP Range, up to 8 times) when the server allows it, and a file shorter than the server announced is never kept. The SOIR file now arrives whole (identical to a separately resumed copy) and its 644 profiles are indexed.
- **Fix: plot toolbar over the titles.** In the altitude cut, spectra and cross-section tabs the Plotly toolbar covered the plot title in a 1024 px window; the titles now sit just above the plot area, below the toolbar.
- **Fix: Mars Express and MRO profiles drawn in the same colour.** Missions without a colour of their own took the palette's first colour (#38bdf8), almost MRO's (#40c4ff), so in a Mars comparison the MEX and MRO profiles could not be told apart. Mars Express, MGS, Odyssey, MSL, InSight, Phoenix, MER and VEGA now have their own colours, and MOM's no longer resembles MAVEN's (checked in dark and light themes at 1024-1920 px).
- **Fix: catalogue connections left open.** Every catalogue query opened an SQLite connection that was committed but never closed until Python's garbage collector found it (ResourceWarning: unclosed database, many per session; on Windows an open connection also keeps the database file locked). Connections are now closed when each query ends; the test suite runs without these warnings.
- **Fix: one file downloaded twice at the same time.** Products in one archive folder share format files, and selected products are downloaded several at a time (profiles in several worker processes): both downloads wrote the same temporary file, so one failed with a permission error (the product was left out) and the saved file could be cut short. Each download now writes its own temporary file, and a file left half-written by a full disk or a cancelled download is removed.

### Choosing and comparing profiles
- **Fix: dates in other forms selected the wrong profiles.** Comparison and search dates are compared with the profiles' times as text, so a date such as 2020-1-31 (or 2020-02-30) was accepted and selected the wrong profiles; dates must now be YYYY-MM-DD. *Export profiles, all variables* makes the same checks as the comparison (start after end, unknown grouping).
- **Filters by coverage, resolution, uncertainty, data set and instrument**: *Which profiles* can require that the compared variable covers an altitude range, a largest median level spacing, and a largest median 1-sigma (absolute or in percent), and can restrict the data sets and instruments drawn from. The selection report names the filters in force and what each left out; the recipe line records the filter.
- **Outlier screen**: profiles far from the others (robust z per level against the median and MAD, within their group when grouped) are flagged, drawn dashed and listed in the status line and the Comparison CSV; they are left out of the composites only when asked, and the recipe line records the choice.
- **Which profiles** are compared is chosen in the comparison itself (introduced in 0.1.0) and now also by Mars season: an Ls range (330 to 30 wraps through the equinox). Profiles picked by hand (**Compare selected**) are read in the worker processes too (they were read one at a time), and a product that cannot be downloaded or read is left out instead of failing the whole comparison.
- **Pressure levels**: *Vertical* > *Pressure* compares on a grid uniform in log pressure (0.02 decades) instead of altitude, so profiles with different altitude references (1-bar level, ellipsoid, landing site, sphere) line up; profiles without pressure are left out. The CSV (first column `pressure_hpa`) and the publication figure (log pressure axis) follow.
- **Grid step**: the spacing of the common altitude grid can be chosen (*Grid step*, 0.01 to 100 km; empty keeps the default, 0.5 km on Venus, Mars and Pluto and 2 km elsewhere). The step is in the comparison CSV header.
- **Standard error of the mean and inverse-variance weighting.** Composites (overall and per group) give the standard error of the mean, spread / sqrt(n_eff), drawn as a darker band around the mean, and the effective number of profiles at each level; *Weighting: Inverse variance* weights each value by 1/sigma^2 from its own uncertainty (n_eff = V1^2/V2). Both are in the comparison CSV, the recipe line and the publication figure.
- **Bootstrap intervals of the mean**: the 95 % percentile bootstrap interval of the composite and of each group mean (profiles resampled 1000 times with the same weights), shown as the band around the mean when chosen under *Band around the mean* and written as `ci95_low`/`ci95_high` columns.
- **Group composites (climatologies)** by latitude band, local solar time, solar zenith angle, Mars season Ls, year, month, month of the year or mission, each with its own mean and spread. The publication figure follows the grouping: group means with their own spread over the faded profiles; loaded files are labelled with the mission they came from.
- **Deviation view**: each profile minus its group (or composite) mean, in percent for pressure and densities.
- **Comparison choices are remembered**: the *Which profiles* fields, grouping, grid step, vertical coordinate and view are filled in again when VEDA opens (a remembered filter is used only after *Apply*, so nothing is downloaded unasked).
- **Open comparison**: the Comparison CSV records the comparison (settings and the exact profiles used) on its second line, `# recipe: {...}`, and *Open comparison (from its CSV)* redoes it from the file; the same JSON can be posted to the comparison API.
- The comparison variable list offers H2SO4 vapour and microwave absorptivity (Magellan) only for Venus; on other bodies they always gave an empty comparison. The H2SO4 entry no longer shows its unit twice.
- The *Which profiles* report names your loaded files "Your loaded files" instead of the internal "USER_IMPORTED".
- **Fix: profiles without the compared variable used up a comparison.** Filtered comparisons drew their candidates from every profile data set of a mission, so a Magellan temperature comparison spent part of its *Profiles per mission* on the H2SO4 and absorptivity profiles (which hold no temperature) and returned fewer profiles than asked. Candidates now come only from data sets that hold the variable (quantities only some data sets publish, such as H2SO4 or the measured mass density, only from those).
- **Fix: comparisons did not say that a data set was not indexed when its bundled sample was catalogued.** A date-range comparison of Mars Express (or Akatsuki, Cassini Titan ionosphere) on a new data folder found only the sample shipped with VEDA and reported "1 kept" without the usual "not indexed yet" note, as if the archive held nothing else; a data set now counts as indexed only once an archive volume has been indexed.
- **Fix: "downloaded only" missed repository data sets.** The Venus Express VeRa, FSI and SOIR profiles were never found by *Downloaded only* searches, by comparisons with *Download* unticked, or by the default comparison (they are kept as converted CSV files, not PDS labels), although the tables showed them as downloaded.
- **Fix: an older comparison could replace a newer one.** Changing the variable (or any option) while a comparison was still running drew whichever answer came back last: temperatures in degrees Celsius could appear under a pressure axis. Answers to superseded requests are now dropped, and the *Which profiles* report shows *Searching...* while a filtered search runs instead of the previous result.
- **Fix: log-space composites mixed logarithms and values.** If a later profile in a pressure, density or electron density comparison had a zero or negative value (noisy electron densities often do), the earlier profiles had already been interpolated as logarithms and the rest as values: two profiles of 10^4 cm^-3 averaged to 5005, the first was drawn at 9.2 (its logarithm), and the result depended on the order of the profiles. Log or linear averaging is now decided once for all profiles.
- **Uncertainty budget of a comparison.** *Show: Uncertainty budget* gives at each level the spread between the profiles, the part their random errors explain (spread^2 = variability^2 + mean sigma^2, over the profiles with a 1-sigma) and the natural variability left, the profiles' systematic uncertainty, and the standard error of the mean against its random error and its systematic error (from independent between profiles to shared by all); the Comparison CSV gets `budget_*` columns. Checked on synthetic profiles (5 K variability, 3 K errors: 5.0 and 3.0 K recovered from 4000 profiles). 26 Mars Express profiles: the random errors explain under 1 % of the variance below 15 km and 5 to 14 % at 25-45 km, where the systematic error of the mean, 3 to 7 K (independent) to 12 to 16 K (shared), far exceeds its random error of 1 to 2 K.
- **Sampling of a comparison.** A *Sampling* tab beside the soundings shows where and when the compared profiles were taken: latitude against the season (Mars solar longitude Ls; the date elsewhere) and against local solar time, one point per profile, coloured by mission or by group; a click opens the profile. Every point is the profile's own latitude, Ls and local time (checked for 56 Mars Express and MGS profiles, 112 points). It shows, for example, that MGS observed at 3 to 14 h local time (half between 3 and 5 h) and Mars Express at 4 to 21 h (96 % between 9 and 21 h), so differences between the two can be local-time differences.
- **Smoothing to a common vertical resolution.** *Smooth to (km)* smooths each compared profile on the grid before averaging, with a Gaussian of that full width at half maximum and weights 1/sigma^2 (noisy levels count less), and propagates its 1-sigma with the errors' correlation between levels, taken at the scale of the window from the profile's own residual about its smoothed version: the level-to-level correlation would have made smoothed Mars Express, MGS and MRO profiles look up to three times more precise than they are (their residuals are 0.19-0.40 of what it predicts; matched lengths 1.25-2.21 km against 0.47-1.61 km). Checked on synthetic correlated errors (lengths 0.3, 1 and 3 km found as 0.30, 1.02 and 3.07 km; smoothed 1-sigma within 1 % of Monte Carlo). The systematic uncertainty is smoothed with the same weights; the Comparison CSV and its recipe record the smoothing; altitude levels only.
- **MCS dust and water-ice opacity profiles.** The opacity per km on each pressure surface of the MCS rows, dust at 21.6 microns and water ice at 11.9 microns with their 1-sigma, are comparison and profile variables (*Dust opacity per km, MCS*, *Water-ice opacity per km, MCS*) and are in the CSV exports. December 2011 within 30 degrees of the equator: water ice peaks at 3.1e-3 per km near 30 km (the aphelion cloud belt); dust falls from 1.0e-3 per km at 20 km to 1e-6 at 40 km.
- **Group by mission and instrument.** *Group composites by: Mission and instrument* keeps one mission's instruments apart, so *Difference between groups* compares, for example, MRO's Mars Climate Sounder with its radio occultations: December 2011, 62-68 deg N, 3-4 h, on pressure levels, MCS is 3.2 +- 0.8 K (95 %: 1.8 to 4.8) warmer at 2 hPa and 4.0 +- 0.9 K warmer at 0.1 hPa, the same as the difference of the two separate composites.
- **Coincident pairs.** *Show: Difference of coincident pairs* pairs the profiles of the first group with those of each other group taken close in time and place (by default within 12 h, 5 deg of latitude, 20 deg of longitude and 1.5 h of local time; closest first, each profile once) and plots the mean of the pairs' differences with its standard error, a 95 % bootstrap interval and each pair's difference, so that two instruments are compared on the same atmosphere rather than on their sampling. The Comparison CSV gets the pairs' columns and the list of pairs, and the recipe keeps the tolerances. In December 2011 each of the 26 MRO radio occultations has a Mars Climate Sounder profile within 0.25 h (median 0.01 h); on pressure levels the occultations are 2.1 +- 0.4 K warmer at 5 hPa and 1.2 +- 0.5 to 2.8 +- 0.8 K colder at 2 to 0.1 hPa. The two groups' composites of the same profiles give the same means with standard errors up to 3 times larger. Checked against pairs chosen by hand and a separate interpolation in log pressure (the same pairs, means and standard errors). Times written with a UTC offset (+00:00, as in loaded files) pair with those without; such a pair stopped the comparison with an error before.

### Time, local time and season of each profile
- **Local time, solar zenith angle and Mars season (Ls) for every profile.** Where the archive does not give them (Mars Express, Venus Express SOIR zenith angles), VEDA computes them from the time and position with the JPL planetary elements and IAU rotation models; they agree with the archives that publish them to 0.01 h and 0.05 deg. Mars Express profiles were left out of every local-time or zenith-angle filter and group before ("local time unknown"). Ls is a filter, a grouping, an altitude-cut axis and a column of both CSV exports.
- **Measurement times for Mars Express, MRO and MGS.** Their label times are ground-station or pass times, up to 21 minutes from the measurement; VEDA now uses the spacecraft (MRO) and occultation (MGS) times from the header tables, which were ignored along with their local time, zenith angle and Ls, and subtracts the light time from Mars Express sample times.
- **Fix: profiles crossing midnight or the 0/360 meridian.** A profile's local time and longitude were the plain median along the ray path, so a profile from 23.9 h to 0.1 h was placed at 12 h (in local-time filters, groups and the altitude cut) and one crossing longitude 0 at 180 (also in loaded files). They are now medians taken around the circle. The Akatsuki sample profile (Workflow Guide demonstration) is treated the same way, like the archive profiles, and no longer falls back to a fixed time when a label has none.

### Altitude cut, layer statistics and fits
- **Reference atmosphere for Mars**: the Mars-GRAM 2024 global-average profile (-8 to 80 km, dust optical depth 0.3; table from NASA's Aviary project, Apache License 2.0) on Mars comparisons, as for Venus. Its altitudes are above the MOLA areoid, up to 7 km above and 13 km below the sphere VEDA's Mars profiles use, so the status line advises pressure levels; its pressures were checked to be hydrostatic for geometric heights (the table's documentation says geopotential). On pressure levels 10 low-latitude Mars Express profiles are within 3 K of it from 3 to 0.1 hPa. The Mars Climate Database is not offered: its terms exclude commercial use without authorisation, which VEDA's MIT licence could not pass on.
- **Reference atmosphere for Venus**: the Venus-GRAM 2021 global-average profile (VIRA below 100 km, 0-150 km; table from NASA's Aviary project, Apache License 2.0) drawn on comparisons, with a *Deviation from the reference atmosphere* view and a CSV column.
- **Vertical wavenumber spectra**: a *Spectra* tab with the spectra of normalised temperature perturbations of the compared profiles in a layer (quadratic background, Hann window), their mean with a bootstrap interval, the fitted log-log slope with its uncertainty and an m^-3 reference line, and a CSV export (also `POST /api/veda/analysis/vertical-spectra/{body}`).
- **Latitude-altitude cross section**: a *Cross section* tab with the zonal mean of the compared profiles in latitude bands at every level, its standard error and the number of profiles, as a map, and a CSV export with the recipe line.
- **Altitude cut**: the compared variable at one altitude against time, latitude, local time, zenith angle, longitude or day of year, one point per profile, coloured by mission, group, latitude or local time; click a point to open its profile.
- **Layer statistics and profile diagnostics.** *Show* plots, one point per profile, the minimum, maximum or mean of the compared variable between two altitudes, the altitude of the minimum or maximum, or a quantity computed from each whole profile: cold-point tropopause, electron density peak, Chapman layer fit, electron content, gravity-wave potential energy and wavelength, hydrostatic consistency and the top temperature of the retrieval from density. These were computed for every profile but shown nowhere; they are now also in the comparison CSV.
- **Tides and waves**: against local time, longitude or Ls, *Fit* draws a least-squares fit of a mean and one to four harmonics of the period through the points and lists each harmonic's amplitude and the position of its maximum with 1-sigma uncertainties (densities and pressure in percent of the mean, fitted in log), with a warning where the data leave gaps too wide to resolve a harmonic; also available as `POST /api/veda/analysis/harmonic-fit`.
- **Correlation and regression between any two quantities**: the altitude cut can plot the compared variable or a whole-profile quantity against the variable at another altitude or another whole-profile quantity, with Pearson r (Fisher-z interval, p-value), Spearman rho (bootstrap interval) and the least-squares line with t and bootstrap intervals of its slope (also `POST /api/veda/analysis/correlation`).
- **Statistics of the altitude-cut points**: mean with standard error and 95 % bootstrap intervals of the mean and median, overall and per mission or group, under the cut (also `POST /api/veda/analysis/point-statistics`); each point shows its own 1-sigma as an error bar (propagated for layer means).
- **Fix: 1-sigma of layer means too small, and of values between levels too large.** A layer mean's error bar took the comparison's grid values as independent, but values interpolated between the same two levels of a profile share their errors: on a 0.1 km grid the error bars of 2-10 km layer means of real Mars Express profiles were 0.2-0.8 times (median 0.47) the exact propagation through the interpolation. Each profile in a comparison now carries a correlation length of its errors on the grid (`sigma_correlation_km`: that of the errors at its levels, combined with that of the interpolation, level spacing / sqrt 3), and the layer mean adds the correlated terms; on the same profiles it is within 0.86-1.14 of the exact value on 0.1-1 km grids. The 1-sigma of a value between two levels is now that of the interpolated value, sqrt((1-w)^2 s_a^2 + w^2 s_b^2 + 2 w (1-w) r s_a s_b), not the interpolated 1-sigma, which assumed fully correlated levels and was up to 1.41 times too large midway between independent ones.
- **Binned means in the altitude cut**: *Bins* (a width in the units of the x axis) draws the mean of the points in each bin along Ls, local time, latitude, zenith angle, longitude, day of year or another quantity, with its 95 % bootstrap interval, at the mean x of the bin's points, and lists each bin's mean, interval and number of profiles; bins along local time, longitude and Ls wrap around (also `POST /api/veda/analysis/binned-statistics`). Example: 186 MRO radio occultation profiles at 20 km in 60-degree Ls bins: 150.9 K [150.3, 151.5] (90 profiles), 166.2 K [162.9, 169.5] (59), 179.7 K [177.9, 181.4] (37), the same means as computed directly.
- **Tide and wave fits with uncertainties of the points and bootstrap intervals**: when every point of the altitude cut has a 1-sigma, the fit is weighted by 1/sigma^2 and its covariance scaled by the reduced chi-square when that exceeds 1 (shown); each amplitude and position of the maximum also gets a 95 % bootstrap interval (1000 refits of resampled points, positions taken around the circle). The fit endpoint takes `y_sigma` and `bootstrap`.

### Derived quantities and physics
- **Temperature from density only over at least one scale height.** A profile over which the density falls by less than a factor e from its bottom to its top gets no temperature from density: every level of it would depend on the fitted boundary temperature (a few kilometres of a MAVEN accelerometer pass near periapsis gave 4 to 7 K). None of the 329 archive profiles with a temperature from density in a test cache (radio occultations, accelerometer, entry and Titan profiles) changes.
- **Uncertainties of derived quantities.** The archived 1-sigma uncertainties are carried into the scale height, mass density, potential temperature and speed of sound (first-order formulas) and into the lapse rate, N^2, d(theta)/dz and the temperature and pressure retrieved from density profiles (Monte Carlo, 200 draws, through the same derivatives and retrieval as the values; errors correlated between levels as their own scatter shows, see below). They are drawn as bands in the profile view, around each profile of a comparison of up to eight profiles and in the publication figure, and written as `sigma_*` columns in the profile, comparison and all-variables CSVs (User Guide, section 3). SOIR density uncertainties are now read, so the SOIR temperature retrieval has one.
- **Systematic uncertainty, kept apart from the random 1-sigma.** Mars Express and Akatsuki radio occultation profiles are integrated down from an assumed temperature at the top, and the archives give the retrievals for a low, middle and high value (130/165/200 K and 140/170/200 K); VEDA used the middle one and ignored the others. Half the low-high difference is now kept as the systematic uncertainty of temperature and pressure and, recomputed from both retrievals, of every derived quantity: dotted lines beside the random band in profile plots and small comparisons, `systematic_*` columns in the exports. Mars Express sample: 35 K at the top (random 10 K), 8.8 K at 35.8 km, 0.4 K near the ground; pressure 1.08 Pa at every level. Akatsuki, whose own 1-sigma columns are invalid (-9.99), now has an uncertainty: up to 30 K at the top. Pioneer Venus Orbiter temperature profiles, whose three retrievals (150, 200 and 250 K at the top, on the same radii) VEDA used as a random 1-sigma, now carry that spread as systematic too (orbit 18: 50 K at 106.9 km, 9.2 K at 104.2 km), so it no longer weights composites or passes the uncertainty filter as a measurement error.
- **Fix: slow loading of densely sampled entry profiles.** The correlated draws and the 68 % spread added in this version took 96 s to read the 52,201-level MSL entry profile and 49 s for Phoenix (91,835 levels); both steps are now vectorised (identical results; a profile with a long correlation over very many levels uses an even auxiliary grid), and the profiles load in 13 and 17 s, as before these additions (12 and 20 s).
- **Fix: Monte Carlo uncertainties set by a few extreme draws.** The uncertainty of the lapse rate, N^2 and the retrieval from density was the standard deviation of 200 redrawn profiles; where the retrieval reacts strongly to its errors a few draws far out set it (SOIR orbit 433 temperature from density: up to 7988 K, now 158 K). It is now half the width of the central 68 % of the draws, the standard deviation for normal draws: unchanged for Mars Express (0.98-0.99 times, sampling) and most SOIR profiles (median 0.96), up to 50 times smaller in those.
- **Differences between groups with intervals**: with *Group composites by*, *Show: Difference between groups* draws each group's mean minus the first group's at every level, with its standard error and a 95 % bootstrap interval (each group resampled on its own), in percent for pressure and densities; also in the Comparison CSV and the comparison data (`group_differences`). Checked on 169 Mars Express profiles in latitude bands: the same differences and standard errors as a direct Welch computation, intervals about +-1.96 standard errors.
- **Composite weights from the random and systematic uncertainty**: *Weighting: Inverse variance, random + systematic* weights each value by 1/(sigma^2 + s^2), with s the profile's systematic uncertainty where it has one; profiles without a random 1-sigma are left out as with 1/sigma^2. Real example, 14 Mars Express and 19 MGS low-latitude profiles at 35 km: 166.0 K with 1/sigma^2, where the Mars Express profiles carry 85 % of the weight, and 170.9 K with the systematic added (37 %): at that height the Mars Express systematic uncertainty is several times its random 1-sigma (8.8 against 2.5 K in the sample profile). Also in comparison requests (`weighting: inverse_variance_total`) and recipes.
- **Publication figure with the systematic uncertainty**: in a figure of up to eight profiles, each profile's systematic uncertainty (the boundary temperature of Mars Express, Akatsuki and Pioneer Venus retrievals) is drawn as dotted lines beside its shaded random 1-sigma, as on screen.
- **CO2 frost point on Mars and Venus**: for every profile with temperature and pressure, the frost-point temperature of CO2 at each level (James et al. 1992, with the CO2 partial pressure) and the margin of the temperature above it, with 1-sigma, as variables in the profile view, comparisons and exports, and each profile's smallest margin, its altitude and 1-sigma as per-profile quantities in the altitude cut. Checked on archive profiles: Mars Express (northern winter) and MGS (southern winter 1999) polar profiles come within -2.4 to -0.4 K of the frost point near the ground, low-latitude profiles stay 19 K or more above it; SOIR's smallest margins are at 120-130 km, the cold layer reported by Mahieux et al. (2012).
- **Fix: SOIR mass density and temperature from density with a molar mass the upper atmosphere does not have.** VEDA used Venus' bulk molar mass (43.4 g/mol) at every level of the SOIR profiles, which reach 175 km where carbon dioxide gives way to atomic oxygen and carbon monoxide; the archive gives the mean molar mass at each level (`total_molar_mass`, about 22 g/mol at 175 km), now read and used for the mass density, scale height, speed of sound, the temperature retrieved from the total density and the hydrostatic check. Over the 644 profiles the mass density was up to 1.95 times too high (median of each profile's largest error 4.5 %), and the temperature from density, 10 km or more below each profile's top, was 9 to 47 % above the archive's temperature at 130-170 km (medians by 10-km band); it is now 3 to 15 % below it there and 1 to 3 % below at 100-130 km, where the archive's own pressures and temperatures imply a molar mass 2 to 6 % above its molar mass column. The hydrostatic check of profiles reaching above 160 km falls from a median largest departure of 88 % to 21 %. The molar mass is shown as *Mean molar mass (archive)*; SOIR files already downloaded are rewritten when next read.
- **Fix: no uncertainty for the number densities of Mars Express, MGS and MRO.** Their tables give SIGMA NUMBER DENSITY, which VEDA did not map for MGS and MRO and skipped for Mars Express, because the same table has LOWER / MEDIUM / UPPER variants of the temperature and pressure columns and only MEDIUM ones were accepted. It is read now (0.21, 0.53 and 0.35 % of the density, medians), so number-density comparisons of these missions have uncertainty bands, inverse-variance weights and an uncertainty budget, and the temperature retrieved from the density has a Monte Carlo 1-sigma: 0.82, 0.83 and 0.64 times the archive's temperature 1-sigma (medians of 26, 30 and 12 profiles).
- **Fix: data set citations that did not match their DOI records.** Checked every reference against Crossref and DataCite: the SPICAV-SOIR repository is by Vandaele, Mahieux, Wilquet, Chamberlain and Trompet (2017), not "BIRA-IASB (2020)"; the Cassini Saturn ionosphere and UVIS thermosphere bundles were registered in 2019 (cited 2014 and 2018); the InSight EDL bundle lists only Karatekin and the MSL EDL bundle Withers first; the bundles carry their registered titles, and the Galileo mission overview its title in Space Science Reviews.
- **Cite lists the method papers.** Using the Venus or Mars reference atmosphere, the CO2 frost point, derived quantities of Mars profiles (Mars' gravity field) or of Titan profiles (real gas) now adds their papers to the Cite panel under *Methods*: Justh et al. 2021 (Venus-GRAM, NASA/TM-20210022168) and Seiff et al. 1985 (VIRA), Justh et al. 2024 (Mars-GRAM 2024, NASA/TM-20240012934), James et al. 1992 and Greve et al. 2010, Konopliv et al. 2016 (MRO120D) and Tsonopoulos 1974, with BibTeX; the journal records were checked on Crossref and the memoranda on the NASA Technical Reports Server.
- **Fix: Titan's troposphere taken as an ideal gas.** At 70-95 K and 1.5 bar nitrogen is 4 % denser than an ideal gas (p = Z n k T, Z = 0.96 at the surface): VEDA now computes Z from the second virial coefficient of the N2-CH4 mixture (Tsonopoulos 1974) and uses it for the mass density, the scale height, the hydrostatic check and the temperature retrieved from a density. The Cassini radio occultation archive, which gives p, n and T, implies Z = 0.958-0.964 at the lowest level of its 20 profiles; VEDA's is within 0.2 % of it below 60 km. Over those profiles the mass density at the lowest level went from 0.960 to 0.997 times the archive's, the temperature from density from 0.959 to 0.996 times the archive's, and the hydrostatic check's median largest departure from 7.6 % to 1.9 %.
- **Fix: Titan's speed of sound too fast near the surface.** The speed of sound used the ideal gas, gamma R T, where Titan's cold dense nitrogen is not ideal: it is now corrected with the acoustic virial coefficient from the same second virial coefficient (Gillis & Moldover 1996), 4.2 m/s (2.1 %) lower at the surface (94 K, 1.47 bar). For nitrogen at 80, 94 and 100 K VEDA now gives 177.9, 193.7 and 201.6 m/s against NIST's 178.3, 193.8 and 201.6 (the ideal gas: 182.3, 197.6 and 203.8).
- **Fix: Mars gravity the same at every latitude.** VEDA used one surface gravity for Mars (3.72 m/s^2 at 3389.5 km), but its flattening (J2) and rotation make it 3.722 m/s^2 at the equator and 3.706 at the poles on that sphere (3.707 and 3.735 on the surface ellipsoid). Mars profiles with a latitude now use the effective gravity of the MRO120D field (Konopliv et al. 2016: GM 42828.375 km^3/s^2, J2 1.956608e-3 at 3396.0 km) with the centrifugal term, as for Jupiter and Saturn: scale heights and the dry adiabatic lapse rate change by up to 0.4 %, N^2 by up to 0.8 %. Mars Express integrates its pressures with this gravity: over 26 profiles its hydrostatic check fell from a median largest departure of 0.76 % to 0.08 %, and the temperature integrated from its density and top temperature matches its own within 0.033 % instead of 0.29 %. The Phoenix entry profile (68 deg N) is reproduced with a median difference of 0.0 K instead of +0.5 K, and its largest hydrostatic departure is 1.3 % instead of 5.0 %.
- **Fix: MGS and MRO hydrostatic check and temperature from density off by up to 2 %.** These archives (D. Hinson) integrate their pressures in the geopotential they give at each tangent point (column GEOPOTENTIAL, from a full gravity field with the planet's rotation), and the tangent point drifts in latitude along a profile (MGS 9170R28A: 0.03 deg per 330 m of height), so the geopotential rose 2.2 % more slowly with height than the spherical gravity g(z) VEDA integrated with. VEDA now integrates in that geopotential for these two data sets. Over 30 MGS profiles the hydrostatic check fell from a median largest departure of 1.46 % (90th percentile 2.6 %) to 0.056 % (0.36 %), over 12 MRO profiles from 0.70 % to 0.033 %; integrated down from the archive's own top temperature, the temperature from the archive density now matches the archive's within 0.03 % (MGS, median of each profile's largest difference; 0.68 % before) and 0.011 % (MRO; 0.27 %). Mars Express gives the column too but integrates with the local gravity at the tangent point (its own temperatures follow from that, not from the column, for profiles whose tangent point drifts), and Akatsuki's column is GM/r, so neither changes.
- **Fix: Saturn thermosphere temperatures from density 14 % too high.** The Cassini UVIS profiles give the density of molecular hydrogen alone ("molecular hydrogen number density", the labels), but VEDA's hydrostatic retrieval used Saturn's bulk molar mass (2.30 g/mol, with helium) for its scale height. It now uses that of H2 (2.016 g/mol) for these profiles: over 25 real profiles the temperature from density was 1.135 times the archive's own retrieved temperature (1.125-1.144) and is now 0.996 (0.987-1.004).
- **Fix: errors taken as independent between levels.** No archive says how its errors are correlated, and VEDA took them as independent, but real profiles scatter far less between neighbouring levels than independent errors of the archived size would make them: the second differences of Mars Express temperatures have 5 % of that variance (median of 32 profiles), those of SOIR 0.1 % (60 profiles). VEDA now finds for each profile and each of temperature, pressure and density the shortest Gaussian correlation length that its scatter allows (a lower bound; Mars Express temperatures 0.26-1.36 km, median 0.52 km, near the 0.3-0.65 km Fresnel radius the files give; SOIR 3.8-10.6 km, about a scale height) and redraws the errors with it. Uncertainties of differences of levels fall (lapse rate and N^2: Mars Express 0.72 times, SOIR 0.27 times the previous value, medians), those of sums rise (pressure integrated from SOIR densities: 2.7 times; layer means in the altitude cut, which now use the same length). The lengths are listed with the profile's attributes; a data set can still declare one.
- **Fix: electron densities taken for neutral densities.** In Mars Express ionospheric occultation files (IID), which have an ELECTRON NUMBER DENSITY column and no neutral one, the search for NUMBER DENSITY found the electron column: these profiles gained a neutral number density, a mass density and a temperature "from density" of thousands to millions of kelvin (M43TNFXL04_IID_221721851_60: median 11,650 K), which entered comparisons of those variables. The electron column is no longer used for it.
- Pressures that a label gives in kPa, microbar, nanobar or dyn/cm^2 are converted; they were all taken for pascals (no data set VEDA reads uses them today; archive labels can).
- **Fix: a failed Chapman fit was shown as a fit.** When the Chapman layer fit did not converge, the measured electron density peak and its altitude were reported as the fitted peak (in the altitude cut and the comparison CSV); a failed fit now gives no Chapman values, and the measured peak is still given as such.
- **Temperature and pressure from density profiles.** Profiles with a measured mass density (the MRO and Mars Odyssey accelerometers, the Phoenix, Spirit and Opportunity entry profiles) or a total number density (Venus Express SOIR, radio occultations) gain a temperature and a pressure from downward hydrostatic integration, with the upper boundary from an isothermal fit to the top of the profile (User Guide, section 3). They are new comparison and profile variables; until now the accelerometer profiles had a density and nothing derived from it. Checked against the Phoenix entry temperatures (within 1 K from 10 to 100 km) and the SOIR temperatures (1 K on average below 125 km); above the homopause the body's mean molar mass makes the temperatures too warm.
- **Hydrostatic consistency of each profile**: for profiles with temperature and pressure, the largest and median departure (in percent) of the archived pressure from the pressure integrated hydrostatically with the profile's own temperature, and the altitude of the largest. The radio occultation samples give 0.1 % (Mars Express) and 0.4 % (Akatsuki).
- The hydrostatic consistency check gives the departures expected from a profile's errors alone (median and 95th percentile of the largest, Monte Carlo with a perfectly hydrostatic profile redrawn within the archived errors), as diagnostics beside the measured departures.
- **Gravity on Jupiter and Saturn depends on latitude**: profiles with a latitude use the effective gravity there (J2 of the Juno and Cassini gravity fields and rotation), from 23.1 m/s^2 at Jupiter's equator to 26.9 at its poles and 9.0 to 12.1 on Saturn, instead of 24.79 and 10.44 everywhere. Scale heights, dry adiabatic lapse rates, N^2 and wave energies on these planets change by up to 15 %.
- **Fix: gravity-wave background and potential energy.** The background temperature came from an 8 km Savitzky-Golay window, which left only 58 % of a 6 km wave and 24 % of an 8 km wave in T' (and 120 % of a 4 km wave); it is now a zero-phase Butterworth low-pass with an 8 km cutoff (T' keeps 98 % of waves shorter than 5 km, half of an 8 km wave, under 4 % of waves longer than 12 km). E_p used the measured N^2, which contains the wave and was clipped to 1e-6 s^-2 where the wave made it negative, giving spikes of thousands of J/kg; it now uses the N^2 of the background and is left out where that is unstable. The dominant wavelength is searched only among the waves T' keeps. Mean wave energies and wavelengths change accordingly.
- **Fix: gravity-wave perturbations upside down for profiles listed from the top.** T' and the wave potential energy were computed in altitude order but written back in the file's order, so occultation profiles stored top-down had them reversed in altitude.
- **Fix: tropopause searched at Earth altitudes on every body.** The cold point was looked for between 6 and 25 km, so Titan profiles gave the 25 km edge instead of the 44 km tropopause and Venus profiles (from 40 km) their coldest level. It is now searched where each body has one (Titan by altitude, Jupiter and Saturn by pressure) and only an interior minimum counts; Venus and Mars, which have no cold-point tropopause, report none.
- **Fix: no ionosphere diagnostics without temperature.** Electron content, peak and Chapman fit were skipped for electron density profiles without a temperature column, which is most ionospheric occultations.
- **Fix: electron content too high where a profile is noisy.** Negative electron densities, the noise about zero below and above an ionospheric layer, were set to zero before the electron content was integrated, which keeps only the positive half of the noise (+0.4 sigma per level). They are now integrated as they are: the Cassini Titan profile of 19 March 2006 (T12) gives 0.022 TECU instead of 0.045, the Mars Express profile of 2 April 2004 0.0220 instead of 0.0241. The peak density and its altitude are unchanged; a profile without a positive density has none.
- **Fix: Mars potential temperature with the heat capacity of a 290 K atmosphere.** The exponent R/cp of the potential temperature used a reference cp of 830 J/(kg K), the cp of Mars' CO2 near 290 K, so R/cp was 0.230 against the 0.257 of Mars climate models (cp = 744.5, Forget et al. 1999). The reference cp is now cp(T) of Mars' composition at its 214 K mean surface temperature, 752 J/(kg K) (R/cp = 0.254): potential temperatures at 0.1 hPa are 10 % higher (a factor 61^0.024), at 1 hPa 4.4 %. The lapse rate, N^2 and speed of sound already used cp(T) and do not change.
- **Fix: Chapman fits flattened by noise.** The Chapman layer was fitted to the positive electron densities only, so the noise about zero above and below the layer entered by its positive half and made the fitted layer wider and higher (scale height 3 % too large in simulated profiles with 10 % noise). All levels are fitted now: the Mars Express profile of 2 April 2004 gives a peak at 154.8 km with a 33.6 km scale height instead of 175.2 km (above the profile's top at 158 km) and 47.6 km.

### Body constants from measured compositions
Each body's molar mass, gas constant and heat capacity now follow from a sourced composition; the old values did not match the compositions listed beside them. Effects on derived quantities are given for each.
- **Jupiter (Galileo probe).** Was 2.22 g/mol and R = 3745 J/(kg K) against 2.26 g/mol from its listed composition (89.8 % H2, 10.2 % He, 0.3 % CH4). Now helium 0.1359 of the hydrogen-helium mixture (Helium Interferometer, von Zahn et al. 1998, JGR 103, 22815) and CH4/H2 = 2.38e-3 (mass spectrometer, Wong et al. 2004, Icarus 171, 153), i.e. 86.233 % H2, 13.562 % He and 0.205 % CH4: molar mass 2.3141 g/mol, R = 3593 J/(kg K), cp = 12,092 J/(kg K) (computed as for Saturn). Scale heights 4.1 % smaller, densities computed from pressure and temperature 4.2 % larger, dry adiabatic lapse rate 2.2 % larger.
- **Saturn (Cassini).** Was Voyager's 3.25 % helium by volume, with a molar mass (2.07 g/mol) and gas constant (4016 J/(kg K)) that did not match even that composition (2.14 g/mol). Now 11 % helium (+-2 %, Cassini UVIS occultations and CIRS limb scans, Koskinen & Guerlet 2018, Icarus 307, 161), 0.45 % methane and 88.55 % hydrogen: molar mass 2.2975 g/mol, R = 3619 J/(kg K), cp = 12,276 J/(kg K) (7/2 R for hydrogen, 5/2 R for helium). Scale heights 9.9 % smaller, densities 11 % larger, dry adiabatic lapse rate 14 % larger. (A CIRS-only analysis, Achterberg & Flasar 2020, finds less helium, He/H2 = 0.04-0.075; the value is not settled.)
- **Titan (Huygens GCMS).** Was 28.6 g/mol and R = 290.7 J/(kg K), heavier than pure nitrogen and impossible for its listed nitrogen-methane composition (27.4 g/mol). Now the stratospheric composition of Niemann et al. 2010 (JGR 115, E12006): 1.48 % CH4 (constant above about 45 km; 5.65 % below 7 km), 0.101 % H2 and 98.419 % N2, giving 27.810 g/mol, R = 299.0 J/(kg K) and a reference cp of 1049 J/(kg K). Scale heights 2.8 % larger, densities 2.8 % smaller, cp(T) 2.3 % larger (dry adiabatic lapse rate 2.3 % smaller).
- **Mars (Curiosity SAM).** Was 43.34 g/mol and R = 191.8 J/(kg K) against 43.50 g/mol from its listed composition. Now the three-Mars-year mean of Trainer et al. 2019 (JGR Planets 124, 3000): 95.1 % CO2, 2.59 % N2, 1.94 % Ar, 0.161 % O2, 0.058 % CO, giving 43.487 g/mol and R = 191.2 J/(kg K). Densities 0.3 % larger, scale heights 0.3 % smaller, cp(T) 0.4 % smaller (737 J/(kg K) at 200 K).
- **Pluto (New Horizons, ALMA).** Its listed composition (99 % N2, 0.5 % CH4, 0.1 % CO) added up to 99.6 % and gave 27.95 g/mol against the 28.01 g/mol used. Now 0.30 % CH4 near the surface (New Horizons Alice, 0.28-0.35 %, Young et al. 2018, Icarus 300, 174), 515 ppm CO (ALMA, Lellouch et al. 2017, Icarus 286, 289) and N2 the rest: 27.977 g/mol, R = 297.2 J/(kg K), reference cp 1041 J/(kg K). Derived quantities change by 0.1 %.
- The body banner shows the molar mass and the heat capacity cp beside the gas constant (the user guide said it did; it showed neither). For bodies with temperature-dependent cp the value is marked as the reference.
- The scale heights on the body quick-cards follow from each body's gas constant, gravity and the temperature now stated beside them: Pluto showed 50 km, the value in its warm upper atmosphere, against 17.7 km at its 37 K surface (both now shown); Titan 20.8 km (was 20.0), Mars 11.0 km (was 11.1).
- Ceres and comet 67P: molar mass 18.015 g/mol (water vapour), matching their gas constant of 461.5 J/(kg K); it was 18.0, which gives 461.9. Every body's gas constant is now checked against its molar mass.

### Loading your own files
- **Load dialog**: before a table is read, VEDA shows its columns (unit in the file, first values, range) and asks what each column is and in which unit, and what the file is (body, mission, instrument, observation time). Headerless files can be read. The choices are saved with the file and offered for the next one; loaded profiles carry the mission and instrument in legends and colours and are included in filtered comparisons.
- **Mass density columns** can be loaded (kg/m^3, g/cm^3 or kg/km^3, with a 1-sigma column): files holding only a mass density, such as accelerometer or entry profiles, could not be read at all. They are compared as *Mass density (archive)*. An uncertainty column without a unit in the file is now suggested in its quantity's unit instead of the first unit in the list.
- Header units written as `name [unit]` or `name (unit)` and `# KEY=value` metadata lines are read from text tables.
- **Fix: loaded .FITS and .fts images showed no picture.** Only lower-case .fit and .fits files were rendered; the other accepted FITS names loaded but their image was not found.
- Loading files is limited to 640 MB per request (a 200 MB file and its companions), refused before the request is read into memory; before, the 200 MB limit was checked per file only after the whole request had been read and decoded.
- The observation lists of a mission or body take at most 2000 (mission) or 500 (per mission) entries per request, and archive searches an offset up to ten million; before, any number was accepted.
- **Fix: a failed load could delete a good one.** Loading a file that cannot be read under the name of an earlier loaded file deleted the earlier one before reading the new one, and a failed `obs.tab` deleted a loaded `obs.csv` (both are "obs"). Files are now read first and replace nothing when they fail; a companion file with the main file's name no longer overwrites it, and a file name the file system refuses is reported instead of a server error.
- **Fix: fill values in text tables.** CSV and ASCII files without a label kept -999, -9999 and 1e36 as values: a Kelvin temperature column with a -999 level was taken for degrees Celsius (273 K added to every level), and the fills went into the derived quantities. These values are now missing, as in PDS3 tables, and zero or negative temperatures and pressures are left out when the columns are guessed too.
- **Fixes**: a pressure column in hPa was divided by 100 (the unit test for Pascal also matched "HPA"); loaded files were all given the time 2026-01-01T12:00, which then appeared as an observation time (now the label's time, the one you enter, or none); Celsius was guessed from the median value on some bodies only (now from the label unit, or values below zero).

### Exports
- **Export profiles, all variables**: the compared profiles at their own levels with every archived and derived quantity, uncertainties and per-level geometry, one row per profile and level, for analysis in Python, R or a spreadsheet.
- **Units of every exported column.** The CSV of one profile listed no units, so derived columns such as density, scale_height or buoyancy_freq_sq had none, and the export of all compared profiles had none for the data sets' own variables (the accelerometers' and SOIR's density_measured in kg/m^3, Magellan's H2SO4 in ppm and absorptivity in dB/km, the CRISM aerosol quantities, the Cassini electron content along the ray path). Both now carry a units line for all of them.
- The publication figure's note on differing altitude references no longer covers the x-axis label.
- The CSV exports state each profile's altitude reference: the comparison CSV said "Altitude above the body's reference radius" for every comparison and now names the shared reference (or says the references differ) and lists it per profile; the all-variables CSV gives it beside each profile's source.
- **Fix: comparison CSV wrote small values as zero.** Values had four fixed decimals, so densities (1e-7 kg/m3 in the thermosphere) and pressures above about 60 km on Mars were exported as 0.0000; they now have six significant figures. The CSV also gains the group composites, the number of profiles at each level, and a header line per profile with mission, instrument, time, latitude, longitude, local time and solar zenith angle.
- **Fix: compared data missing from *Cite*.** Only products opened one by one or picked by hand were listed in the Cite panel; profiles that entered a comparison through the filter or the default selection (the usual way) were not, so the panel could say no archive product had been opened under a plotted comparison. Every archive data set in a comparison is now listed.

### Plots and interface
- The profile view's latitude range and the *Colour profiles by* option no longer call every latitude a tangent latitude: accelerometer passes (Mars Odyssey, MRO, MAVEN) and descent profiles give the spacecraft's or probe's position.
- **The status line counts the profiles without values.** Selected profiles with no values of the compared variable (or, on pressure levels, no pressure) were left out without a word, so the selection report could say 8 kept while 6 were drawn (MAVEN ROSE, September 2016: two occultations above the ionosphere). The comparison lists them and the status line says how many.

### Speed
- **Fix: changing the number of workers during a comparison.** Changing Settings > Performance while a comparison was being read cancelled the old worker pool's queued work and the comparison failed with a server error; the rest of it is now read in the main process.
- **Multiprocessing.** Profiles for comparisons are read and derived in parallel worker processes; the number is set in **Settings > Performance** (default: all CPU cores but one; 1 turns it off). The workers start in the background when VEDA opens and are reused, and work is sent in chunks; reading 240 Venus Express profiles took 1.0 s with 4 workers against 5.5 s before.
- **Parallel downloads**: selected products, and the index pages of archive volumes, are fetched several at a time (Settings > Performance, default 4).
- **Reading profiles about twice as fast.** PDS3 labels were parsed one character at a time (Mars radio science labels are 30 to 45 kB, each read twice), and the subsolar point was recomputed for every level of a profile although all levels share one time; together these were 60 % of the time to read a profile. A Mars comparison of 120 downloaded MEX, MGS and MRO profiles took 4.2 s in one process, now 2.2 s.
- **Downloaded products are found from the cache folder listing** instead of one disk check per catalogue row: the default comparison and *Downloaded only* searches took 30 s on a catalogue of 220,000 products, now about 1 s. Catalogue indexes for profile and product-type filters make those searches up to 7 times faster.
- Profiles already read are kept in memory for the session, so re-plotting a comparison with another variable, grouping or unit is immediate.
- **Faster start, no leftover temporary folders.** The app gave Matplotlib a new temporary folder on every start, so it rebuilt its font list each time and every closed or crashed process (including each worker) left a folder in the temp directory (8 per launch). It now keeps one in VEDA's cache.

### Milestone 5 (2026-10-02): reconciliation and scientific corrections

#### Science
- **Temperature-dependent heat capacity** for Venus, Mars, Titan and Pluto: cp(T) from the JANAF ideal-gas tables of the main constituents, weighted by composition (Mars 740 J/(kg K) at 200 K instead of a fixed 830; Venus 850 at 300 K rising to 1140 at 735 K). Used for N^2, the new dry adiabatic lapse rate g/cp(T) and the speed of sound; potential temperature keeps the conventional constant kappa at the reference cp.
- N^2, buoyancy period and dry adiabatic lapse rate are computed from temperature alone (they were skipped for profiles without a pressure column).
- Removed a second, Earth-constant implementation of potential temperature and N^2 (dry-air R and cp, 1000 hPa) that was not used by the app, so every derived value comes from one body-aware implementation.
- **Comparisons**: pressure, densities and electron density are interpolated and averaged in log space (geometric mean, multiplicative spread); the spread is the sample standard deviation and is shown only where at least two profiles overlap; data gaps are no longer bridged; altitudes below the reference level (Hellas and the northern lowlands lie below the 3389.5 km Mars reference sphere, the Galileo probe went below 1 bar) are kept instead of cut at 0 km, and gravity is computed correctly there.
- **One vertical reference for comparisons**: altitudes are measured from the body's reference sphere for every mission. Magellan RSS altitudes, given above 6052 km, are moved up 0.2 km onto the 6051.8 km Venus reference used for VEX radii. Each profile shows its altitude reference, and a comparison that mixes references (the Galileo probe's 1-bar level, the Huygens landing site) says so.
- **Vertical derivatives** no longer blow up at repeated altitudes: the old guard moved duplicates 1e-6 km apart, which turned any difference into a gradient of order 1e6 K/km. Duplicates are now averaged, and every derivative is a central difference over the neighbouring levels but never over less than +-50 m, so lapse rate and N^2 of entry profiles, and of the VEGA 2 lander after touchdown (altitude jitter of a few metres at constant temperature), are not dominated by noise.
- Zero or negative temperatures, pressures and densities are treated as fill (MER and Phoenix mark missing levels with -1).
- New analytic tests: isothermal Mars (scale height, N^2 = g^2/(cp T), density, sound speed), a true dry adiabat through the deep Venus atmosphere (N^2 = 0 with cp(T)), potential temperature at the reference pressure, Chapman-layer TEC.

#### Data sources
- **MRO radio occultation profiles** (`mrors_2001`, MRO-M-RSS-5-TPS-V1.0, D. Hinson): 186 temperature-pressure-density profiles with uncertainties, 2008 to 2012, from the PDS Atmospheres Node; they compare directly with MGS and MEX profiles (same radius-based vertical reference). Cited as Hinson et al. (2008), Icarus 193, 125-138.

InSight's altitudes are read from its radial distance, because its altitude column is above 3396.19 km rather than the 3389.5 km Mars reference used everywhere else.

Profiles come from the whole archive catalogue, spread evenly over the dates, downloaded when needed, and filtered on their geometry once read; a per-mission report says how many were found, read, kept and why others were left out. - **Mission catalogue**: counts per category, a target-body filter and a search box; landers, rovers and probes have their own badge instead of "Orbiter". - Four lander missions added: MER, Phoenix, MSL and InSight.

- New indexed data sets: all Akatsuki cameras (UVI, IR1, IR2, LIR: raw, calibrated, geometry), MRO MCS DDR/EDR/RDR from cumulative indexes, CTX and MARCI on request, JunoCam, Juno magnetometer, Cassini INMS, Pioneer Venus ONMS. 141 data sets in all.
- Index files are read four at a time; cumulative indexes are streamed from disk.

#### Plot any product
- New reader for PDS3 (ASCII and binary tables, containers, record arrays, multi-line records, images, qubes, arrays), PDS4 (binary, character and delimited tables, arrays), FITS and netCDF, memory-mapped.
- Product viewer: fields against fields and time, spectrograms, profiles per row (MCS), images and maps with stretch, transects and value read-out, cubes with per-pixel spectra. Peak-preserving decimation for large tables.
- Tolerant of common label errors (record lengths, integers labelled as floats, files shorter than their label) and clear messages when a product has no plottable layout.

#### Observation geometry for all missions
- SPICE kernel sources for 17 missions (file-name coverage, PDS3 coverage tables or archive read-me tables); Pluto, Ceres, Vesta and 67P body kernels and frames.
- Kernels download automatically when a mission or observation is opened (Settings: on/off and a size limit).
- Orbit geometry for any observation: 3D orbit, ground track, altitude, local time, solar zenith, emission and phase angles.

#### What to cite
- Cite panel: references for exactly the data sets and features you used (172 references with Crossref-verified DOIs), archive acknowledgements, BibTeX, text and a data availability statement.

#### Speed and stability
- Plots removed from the page are purged (they leaked resize handlers and memory); image-viewer listeners no longer accumulate; no backdrop blur on cards and the top bar.
- Thread-safe publication figures; SQLite WAL; Windows paths kept short for long archive URLs; folder builds instead of a onefile exe (start-up in seconds instead of a minute).

### Milestone 3 (2026-10-01): real archive data only

VEDA now works only with real archive data and can search it by body, mission, payload and date.

#### Breaking
- **All synthetic data removed.** The generated sample granules, the mission adapters that served them and `scripts/generate_sample_granules.py` are gone. Every mission now shows only products from its archive; the bundled samples are unmodified archive products (Akatsuki RS L4 and UVI, Mars Express MaRS L4, Cassini RSS Titan, COSMIC-2).

#### Archive engine
- Reads PDS3 volume indexes (`INDEX.TAB`, format files, DARTS and PSA layouts) and PDS4 bundles into a local SQLite catalogue; products are downloaded on request with retries and atomic writes.
- **Find observations of a body by date** searches every connected data set for that body at once.
- **Archive data** per mission: choose payloads and data sets, filter by date, product type, text, profiles only or downloaded only, sort, download, open and compare.
- Connected data sets: Akatsuki RS L2 to L4 (DARTS); Mars Express MaRS L4 and Venus Express VeRa (PSA); Magellan radio occultation profiles, H2SO4 and raw records, Venus Express VeRa (PDS copy), Mars Global Surveyor RS, Galileo probe, Huygens HASI, Cassini RSS Titan ionosphere (PDS4) and Juno MWR (PDS Atmospheres).
- ISRO ISSDC (Mars Orbiter Mission, Chandrayaan-2): sign in on PRADAN, download there, then import into VEDA.

#### Readers
- PDS3: stack-based label parser with quote-aware tokenising, `^STRUCTURE` format files, multiple tables per file, character columns, record repair, declared missing constants; PDS4 character and delimited tables.
- Unit-aware loading (pressure, temperature, number and electron density including scaled units, radius and altitude in metres); times from the index, label or file name, never from product creation dates.

#### Plotting and analysis
- Multi-panel profiles of every quantity in a product, with the archived 1-sigma uncertainty as bands or error bars, and the product's time, location, SZA and local time.
- **Plot style**: lines, markers, palettes, uncertainty, axes (log, swapped, pressure as vertical axis), fonts, legend, and AGU, Elsevier, A&A and MNRAS templates; **Export figure** at journal column width as PNG or SVG.
- Comparisons coloured by mission, date or latitude.

#### Observation geometry
- SPICE geometry for Akatsuki and Mars Express: orbit (planet-fixed and J2000), view from Earth, tangent-point map in four projections, SZA, local time and Sun-Earth-probe angle along the profile. Kernels are listed with their size and downloaded on confirmation; light time is corrected for Earth-received times.

#### Documentation and terms
- New **TERMS.md** (warranty, responsibility for results, fair use of the archives, privacy). **DATA_POLICY.md** rewritten around the connected data sets, each archive's terms and the reference publications to cite. README, USAGE, third-party licenses (SpiceyPy, NAIF CSPICE, Natural Earth added; Three.js removed, as it is not used), and the in-app Help, About and Data & Licenses panels updated. Data & Licenses lists the connected data sets from the catalogue and offers a copyable data availability statement.
- CSV exports name the source file and VEDA version and state which columns are archived and which are derived.

### Milestone 2 (2026-10-01, briefly tagged v2.1.0): science fixes, stability, security

#### Science fix
- **Akatsuki temperature profile.** Column lookup took the first name that merely
  *contained* the query, so `TEMPERATURE` matched
  `PRESSURE (LOWER TEMPERATURE AT BOUNDARY)` and the Akatsuki radio-science
  profile showed pressure in pascals as temperature (up to 47,300 K). Columns are
  now matched as whole words, preferring the column that leads with the quantity,
  skipping `SIGMA` columns and using the nominal `MEDIUM` retrieval. Akatsuki now
  reads 147-288 K over 54-95 km. No other bundled profile changes. The same fix
  applies to uploaded tables, where a bare `T` could previously match `LATITUDE`.

#### Stability
- The multi-mission comparison crashed on every body when no selected
  observation carried the chosen variable; it now explains what to do.
- Uploaded images opened in the profile viewer and failed; they open in the
  image viewer.
- **Load File** opened the file picker twice and parsed each file twice.
- Uploads are stored one folder per file, so a new upload never reuses a stale
  companion table from an earlier one, and deleting one never breaks another.
- The API returns clear 4xx messages instead of 500 errors for bad figure,
  image and histogram requests, and never includes server paths.
- Image sub-routes (render, histogram) were shadowed by the image route and
  returned 404.
- Three Workflow Guide buttons did nothing.

#### Security
- The local API no longer allows cross-origin requests from other websites and
  checks the `Host` header (DNS rebinding). Set `VEDA_ALLOWED_HOSTS` when serving
  on a network with `veda-server --host 0.0.0.0`.
- Settings are validated and saved atomically; a damaged `settings.json`
  falls back to defaults.

#### Features
- Select or drop several files at once; a PDS3 `.lbl` is sent with its `.tab`.
- Recently loaded files are kept under the *user_imported* mission.
- Settings now all take effect: theme (dark, light, follow system), font size,
  default temperature and pressure units, start-up body and mission,
  publication-figure DPI, online downloads on/off and download timeout,
  plus **Reset to defaults** and links to the data folders.
- The bar / Pa unit buttons now really convert the comparison plot.
- About shows the running version, repository links, a citation for the
  current version (with Copy) and install paths.
- Help is searchable and covers loading files, units, keyboard use and
  troubleshooting.
- Download failures explain the cause (offline, timeout, file removed).

#### Interface
- Plots follow the light/dark theme; the light theme is readable throughout.
- The comparison legend no longer covers the plot title.
- Header fits on one row at laptop widths; fixed the unreadable active tab and
  unit buttons, the squeezed mission list and the observation table.

#### Performance
- Text tables are parsed column-wise with NumPy instead of cell by cell.
